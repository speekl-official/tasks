title: Containment
description: What a decision on a parent does to its subtree — the BACKLOG invariant, the hold, and the cascades.

A parent owns its subtree. Decisions taken on a parent apply to every task beneath it and are
written into each of them, so every file states the truth about its own task; what is *happening*
in the subtree is computed and never stored (see [Situation](/docs/spec/lifecycle#situation)).

Children are parts of the parent's deliverable, never phases of its lifecycle. Designing,
planning, executing, verifying and approving a task happen on the task itself — in `design.md`,
`plan.md` and `state.md`, and through a `handoff` to the approver, whose assignment is the approval
gate. A child named `plan`, `review` or `verify` is a modelling error; an implementation cannot
detect it, so the rule binds authors and the [Agent](/docs/spec/agent-protocol) role.

## Invariant

Every open descendant of a `BACKLOG` task MUST be `BACKLOG`. Validation reports a violation as an
error. The rules below maintain it: nothing under an unscheduled parent can be scheduled, claimed
or reopened past `BACKLOG`, and a parent cannot be deferred around work in flight.

## Hold

A claimed or assigned task **holds** its subtree: while an ancestor is `IN_PROGRESS` or has an
assignee, no task beneath it is ready, and `claim` on one MUST be refused as a conflict naming the
ancestor. Whoever works or must decide on the ancestor comes first; a plan being written or
awaiting approval is not executed. The hold ends when the ancestor is neither claimed nor
assigned: a `handoff` to the approver keeps it, the approver's `unassign` releases it, and an
`assign` back to the author keeps it until the author has reworked and handed off again. Claims
already held beneath are unaffected. An explicit force overrides the hold; it is a judgment, not
an invariant, so validation has nothing to check. An `assign` or `claim` on a task with open
descendants SHOULD report, in its result, how many tasks it now holds, so that a hold is never
taken by accident.

<Callout type="info" title="Non-normative">
  The hold gives the approval flow its gate without a procedural child: the
  planner claims the epic, writes `design.md` and `plan.md`, creates the children — held — and
  hands the epic to the approver; `unassign -m "APPLY"` releases the children to whoever is free,
  `assign` back with a note sends the plan for rework. It also means that an epic assigned to
  someone is an epic that waits on them, and so does everything under it, which makes `assign` the
  gentlest stop of a subtree: new starts cease, work in flight runs to its own pause. "Owning" an
  epic is not expressed by assignment; protection of a subtree against an agent's unilateral
  decisions comes from the [agent protocol](/docs/spec/agent-protocol), which forbids them.
</Callout>

## Cascades

A **cascade** is the part of a transition that applies to the subtree. Three transitions cascade:

| On a task | Each descendant it reaches |
| --- | --- |
| `defer` | every open descendant → `BACKLOG`; `state.md` and `assignee` are kept |
| `schedule` | every `BACKLOG` descendant → `PAUSED` when its `state.md` exists, else `TODO` |
| `cancel` | every open descendant → `CANCELLED`; claims are cleared |

- `done` never cascades: a parent is done only when every child is resolved (the finish gate),
  and nobody can finish a child's work by fiat. `reopen` never cascades either.
- `new` under a `BACKLOG` parent creates the task in `BACKLOG`. `move` of a subtree under a
  `BACKLOG` parent defers the moved subtree as `defer` would.
- `claim` and `schedule` on a task with a `BACKLOG` ancestor MUST be refused as a conflict that
  names the ancestor and `schedule` of it as the remedy; no force overrides this, because the
  remedy is one command.
- **Cascade liveness rule.** A cascade over an `IN_PROGRESS` descendant applies the claimant rule
  of [Authorization](/docs/spec/lifecycle#authorization) to the whole command: a live or unknown claim MUST refuse it,
  naming the claimants; an orphaned claim is superseded and recorded as a takeover would be; an
  explicit force overrides. A `defer` cascade keeps the superseded claim's `state.md`, so
  `schedule` later restores the task to `PAUSED`.
- **Authorization** is checked on the task the command names (see
  [Authorization](/docs/spec/lifecycle#authorization)); descendants are not checked individually.
- **Logging.** The parent's entry names the command and lists the ids the cascade reached; every
  reached descendant gets an entry of its own whose first line is
  `<command> with <parent-id>: <from> → <to>`, followed by the parent's message. History lives
  where the decision was taken and where it took effect.
- **Order and recovery.** A cascade MUST write descendants before the parent, deepest first, so an
  interrupted cascade is completed by repeating the command: descendants already written are
  skipped, and the parent, not yet written, keeps the repeat legal. An interrupted `schedule`
  leaves the invariant violated until the repeat, which validation reports meanwhile.
- **`schedule` is total.** A descendant deferred on its own before its parent was deferred comes
  back with the parent. The decision is in its log; repeating its `defer` is one command, and
  re-deciding it when the parent returns to the plan is what the moment calls for.
- **`finishes_after` is not containment.** A reference there is a join on a task in another
  scope: `cancel` neither cancels it nor waits for it; only `done` waits.
- **A child's cancellation does not cancel the parent.** The parent's deliverable was redefined,
  not lost; the parent stays open until its own `done` or `cancel`.

<Callout type="info" title="Non-normative">
  The model is structured concurrency as Kotlin coroutines define it: a scope
  cannot complete before its children, cancelling the scope cancels every child, a child of a
  not-yet-started scope cannot run, a child that must survive its parent's cancellation is launched
  in another scope — here, moved out first — and cancellation is cooperative: a claimant whose task
  was cancelled or deferred under it finds out at its next command, which conflicts, exactly as
  after a takeover. Two deliberate differences: a child's cancellation is absorbed by the parent (a
  supervisor scope, because an epic that loses one part is redefined, not failed), and the parent's
  own status does not follow its children's activity, because a parent has no claimant and two
  sessions on two children would make any single claimant a lie; activity is shown as `active`
  instead. An earlier draft derived a child's parking from its ancestor and left the child's own
  status untouched, which let a file say `TODO` under an unscheduled epic; the cascade keeps every
  file truthful at the cost of a few extra writes per rare decision.
</Callout>
